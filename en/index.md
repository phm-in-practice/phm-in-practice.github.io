Prognostics and Health Management (PHM) is a framework of technologies that uses sensor signals to diagnose the current condition of a system and predict future failures. Across aerospace, process plants, manufacturing, robotics, and other industries requiring high levels of safety and reliability, PHM is gaining recognition as a key enabler of predictive maintenance.

The authors wrote *PHM in Practice: A Hands-on Guide to Architecting PHM Systems with Real-world Datasets* to promote understanding and wider adoption of PHM. The textbook systematically covers the full PHM workflow, from anomaly detection and fault diagnosis to remaining useful life prediction. It provides real-world datasets collected from industrial equipment and systems, together with code that runs in Google Colab. The accompanying explanations of implementation procedures and result interpretation enable readers to build and test PHM methods themselves. The datasets cover applications ranging from individual components, such as bearings, gears, motors, and batteries, to hydraulic systems, robots, and turbojet engines. The AI methods include ANN, CNN, LSTM, and Transformer architectures, as well as time-series foundation models.

This website, **PHM in Practice**, is an online platform for hands-on learning that extends the textbook’s concepts and methods to new applications. It presents PHM implementation examples beyond those covered in the textbook, providing practical code alongside procedures spanning problem definition, data preprocessing, model development, and result interpretation. Further examples will be added as new datasets become available.

Each example builds on the concepts and methods introduced in the textbook. Readers are therefore encouraged to understand the overall PHM workflow and study the relevant material before starting the exercises. Running the code alone is not enough to understand why a method was chosen, the conditions under which it applies, or the meaning and limitations of its results.
